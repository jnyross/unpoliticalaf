# Impartiality rule for the v3 judge

The judge checks the site's choices against this rule, which is the one the
site already publishes on its methods page ("How we choose what to show"). A
choice that breaks it is a finding. A choice that follows it can still be a
finding if it is misleading in a way the rule does not cover.

## Years

- Headline figures use the publisher's own main measure for the latest year it
  has measured. Forecasts are labelled as forecasts.
- Comparisons over time use fixed windows: ten years for spending by area, and
  the first and latest year of each series elsewhere. A start year is never
  chosen because of the result it gives.
- Comparison pages lead with the same figure whichever way it moved.
- No change is worked out across a break in a series.

## Scales

- Bars start at zero.
- Line charts show their scale on the axis and may start above zero so that
  change is visible; the caption says so where it does.
- Panels that are compared side by side share one scale.
- Cash, after-inflation and share-of-GDP views are labelled as such.

## Ordering and wording

- Groups (age, income fifths, nations, regions, tenures) keep their official
  order, not ranked by size, unless the chart is a ranking and says so.
- Categories in a one-year chart (spending areas, taxes) are ordered by size,
  largest first, or in the publisher's own order.
- Neutral verbs only (rose, fell, higher, lower, changed little). No words that
  judge a figure, and no causes in the site's own words: an explanation is an
  attributed quote from an official body.
- No party, government or politician is named as responsible for a change.

## What counts as material

A finding is **material** if a newcomer reading it would come away with a
different picture of the facts, or a sense of which side is right, that the
official evidence does not support. Wording that is only clumsy, or a choice
that follows the rule and is explained on the page, is not material.
