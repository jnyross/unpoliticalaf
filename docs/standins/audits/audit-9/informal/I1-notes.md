Profile: P3

## What I found out (my words)

- Where the money goes: £1,360bn spent in 2025-26. Biggest bit is pensions/benefits/social care, £33 in every £100, then health £21, then "general public services" £13. Debt interest is its own £11. Roughly a third of everything goes on pensions and benefits, a fifth on the NHS. Found it on the home page and the Spending page.
- Tax as a share of income: 33.6% of gross household income, 2023/24, so about a third. Split 22.9% direct (Income Tax) and 10.7% indirect (VAT). It says plainly this is an average, not my rate. Home page and "Tax and income" page.
- Pay since I was young: the middle full-time worker earns £767 a week before tax (April 2025). After prices, that is about the same as April 2008 (£771 in today's money). So pay has gone nowhere in 17 years. The page says these are survey estimates (a sample of 1% of jobs, give or take £2 a week) and that it does not show why. I can't see how it compares to the 1990s when I was young; the earnings figures I saw only went back to 2008.
- Tax since 1977: 38.1% in 1977 and 33.6% now, but the page says the two methods are not joined, so I can't say if it went up or down properly.

## Things that confused me, slowed me down, or nearly misled me

- Home page: the "Where does public money go?" card says £33 of "every £100 spent on services", but the headline is £1,360bn total; I wasn't sure if the £100 is the whole lot or a bit of it.
- Home page: "General public services (incl. debt interest)" shows £13 but the Spending page says debt interest alone is £11, so I couldn't tell how debt interest can be most of a £13 slice that has other things in it.
- Home page and Spending page: "social protection" is jargon; I had to read the bracket to know it means pensions and benefits.
- Home page: the Who pays card (27.2% by the top 1%) sits next to the household tax card and I nearly mixed the two up; one is Income Tax only, the other is all taxes.
- Home page and Tax and income page: the lowest-income fifth paying 36.7% (more than the middle) looks wrong; the explanation is "for different reasons" and I did not get what those reasons are without more digging.
- Home page: "gross household income" and "direct/indirect taxes" are not explained on the card; they are dotted-underline terms I wasn't sure I could tap.
- Home page (Everyday life): small charts "do not start at zero", so the Earnings and Housing dips look bigger than they are.
- Home page (Housing card): there is a stray little dash to the right of the chart end and the date labels "2008-09" / "2025-26" differ from the "2024-25" in the text.
- Home page and Earnings page: the headline says £767 and the chart says men £800-ish, women £700-ish; I didn't realise at first that £767 is the middle of everyone, not an average.
- Earnings page: "Coding change" labels overlap each other on the chart and are hard to read on a phone.
- Earnings page: the words "amber dashed line before 2011 marks a change (coding change)" mean nothing to me; I don't know what coding is.
- Earnings page: the first thing is a headline about the gap between men and women, when I asked about pay in general; the pay answer was above but the chart under it was a different topic.
- Earnings page: "survey estimates", "margin of error" and "CPIH" are jargon I don't know (CPIH isn't explained where it appears).
- Earnings page: the "More on Pay over time" box and a row of topic buttons with an empty-looking first one (a button with no label) were confusing to tap.
- Earnings page: the page doesn't show pay for the self-employed, and I couldn't tell whether the £767 includes people like me; it says "employee".
- Tax and income page: 33.6% is for "private households", and the page doesn't say what's counted (does it include my self-assessment bill? what about the VAT I pay on the van?).
- Tax and income page: the 1977 history gives two different methods with different numbers for the same year, so I can't trust the trend; it nearly misled me into reading 38.1% to 33.6% as a fall.
- Spending page: the scale line (£19,574 per person, £46,895 per household) is easy to read as what I personally pay; it says it's an average, but only small.
- Spending page: "public sector" and "financial year" are dotted terms and I wasn't sure they could be tapped.
- All pages: a lot of the numbers have no clear "so what", and since I distrust statistics, the "how sure are these figures" link is the first place I wanted to go but it was buried at the end of the pay section.
