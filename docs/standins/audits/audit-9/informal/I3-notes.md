Profile: P5

## What I found out (in my words)

**Where most money goes.** The government spent £1,360bn in 2025-26. Biggest part is "social protection" (pensions, benefits, care): £33 out of every £100. Health is next, £21. Then "general public services" £13, education £10. I found this on the home page and the Spending page. Easy to find.

**Tax on household income.** About 33.6% of gross household income goes on tax, on average (2023/24). That is 22.9% direct tax like Income Tax and 10.7% indirect like VAT. Poorest fifth 36.7%, richest fifth 36.2%. So about one third. I did not find a pounds example (for example, tax on a normal wage).

**Pay since I was young.** Middle full-time worker earns £767 a week before tax (April 2025). After prices rising, that is about the same as April 2008 (£771). So pay has not really grown in 17 years. Men's pay went down then up a bit; women's went up. I did not look at housing, holidays or schools.

## Confusing, slow, or nearly misleading

- Home page, "How much household income goes on tax?": the poorest fifth pays 36.7% and the richest 36.2%, which looks wrong (poor pay more than rich?) and the reason ("for different reasons") is not explained there. Nearly misled me.
- Home page and household-tax page: "fifth of people", "gross household income", "direct" and "indirect" taxes are hard words for me as a second-language reader; the dotted-underline help is small and easy to miss.
- Home page, Spending cards: the bars show £33, £21, £13 but "General public services (incl. debt interest)" at £13 hides that £11 of it is debt interest, so the Spending page's separate "£11 interest" looks like a different, extra amount.
- Spending page: "Health was next with £21" but a different £11 card sits above, so I was unsure which is second biggest.
- Spending page: "£1,360bn" and "per £100 spent on services" are two different bases; it was not clear whether the £33 is of all £1,360bn.
- Home page, Everyday life cards: the small charts have no numbers on the side, so I cannot tell how much pay or housing changed from the card alone.
- Home page, Housing card: it says 65% own their home but shows no earlier figure, so I could not see if that is better or worse than before without tapping in.
- Earnings page, first chart: the two "Coding change" labels overlap and are unreadable; "coding change" itself is jargon.
- Earnings page: the chart headline is about the gap between men and women (£176 to £105), not about pay overall, which I came for; the overall answer is only in the top box.
- Earnings page: "survey estimates", "margin of error" and "Change is not cause" boxes are a lot of words to skim past before the next topic.
- Earnings page: the topic buttons (Work, Earnings, Housing...) wrap onto two lines and numbered tabs are not obvious as navigation.
- Household-tax page, "since 1977": two methods with different numbers for the same year (e.g. 33.8% and 36.3%) and "not joined" are confusing; the chart line has a break and "Method changed" label I did not understand.
- Household-tax page: dates written as "2017/18", "2018/19", "1997/98" mixed with plain years make skimming harder.
- Household-tax page: no example in pounds (for someone on a normal wage), so 33.6% does not tell me what I personally pay.
- Home page, intro to "Everyday life": long paragraph explaining dark dot, gap, dashed line; too much to read before the first number.
