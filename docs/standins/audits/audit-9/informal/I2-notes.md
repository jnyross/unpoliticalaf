**What I found out (in my words)**
- Where most public money goes: the government spent £1,360bn in 2025-26, and the biggest chunk is pensions, benefits and social care, £33 of every £100. Health is £21 and "general public services" (including debt interest) is £13. That was on the home page, no hunting needed.
- How much of a household's income goes on tax: 33.6% of gross household income on average, in 2023/24. So roughly a third. It is split by income fifth, and the poorest fifth is at 36.7%, which surprised me.
- How life has changed (housing, since I was young): 65% of households in England owned their home in 2024-25, and 19% rent privately. In 1981 it was 57% owned, 11% rented privately. Rented privately was 14% in 2008-09. The average private rent is £250 a week and the average mortgage payment is £242. So renting is more common than it used to be, which fits what I see around me.
