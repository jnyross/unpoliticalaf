# Fact check, audit 9: 30 headline claims

Method. I read the built pages in `site/` and the figure records in `site/figures/`, and opened the source files the records name (kept in `data/raw/` and `data/fetched/`) wherever a claim depended on context. I recomputed every calculated figure from its inputs. I could not reach any publisher site: the egress proxy refused www.gov.uk and www.ons.gov.uk with a 403, and the web-search tool had run out of credits. The checks against sources therefore use the copies kept in the repository and the figure records. The "Accuracy check" workflow will download each source again.

Ids are listed in `ids.txt` in the same order as the claims below.

## Other problems found while reading (not in the 30 ids)

- **Minor. Home page, "Everyday life, year by year":** "Every topic has figures from 2008." Holidays have no 2008 figure: the holidays page says "Figures for 2008 are not in the tables used here", and the life index says the earliest year all five topics can be compared is 2009. The statement is false for one topic.
- **Minor. Two pages disagree on when the ETB top-income adjustment starts.** who-pays.html (section 4, Limits) says "From 2001/02 the ONS adjusts for top earners being under-recorded in the survey". household-tax.html says the current method "from 2018/19, adds an estimate of income that very high earners do not report". The two may describe different ONS datasets (Table 16 and the historical quintile series), but as written they contradict each other. I could not resolve this without the publisher's notes.
- **Minor. The 2025-26 spending share of GDP is given two ways.** The spending page gives 44.3% (PESA TME) and a 2018-19 low of 39.1%. The taxes page gives 44.5% (OBR TME) and 39.2%. Both are correct for their source, but only the cash total is labelled "on this basis".

Checked and found correct, not listed above: "spending above receipts in 68 of the 78 years from 1948-49" and "65 of 71 from 1955-56" (recounted from the unrounded OBR data: 68 and 65 are right; three years round to equal); the four- and three-nation "highest/lowest" employment and pay claims; "largest rise Northern Ireland/London" (employment); 17% and −4% for the 10th and 90th percentiles after inflation; "21 OECD countries collected a larger share of GDP in tax than the UK"; GBTS 2025 holidays of 32.1m (page 10 of the toplines PDF, Britain, 2025 column); the private-renting range from 17% (North East) to 29% (London); and the older-method claims about the lowest versus highest fifth.
