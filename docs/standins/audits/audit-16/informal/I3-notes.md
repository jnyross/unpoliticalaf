# I3 notes (30, hairdresser, Swansea, never voted, doesn't know what the Budget is)

## What I found out, in my words

- Where the money goes: the government spends about £1,360bn a year. Out of every £100 on services, £33 goes on pensions, benefits and social care, £21 on health, £13 on "general public services" (£11 of that is interest on debt), £10 on education. Defence is £5. I got this from the first home page card and the Spending page.
- Tax on a household: 33.6% of household income goes on tax on average, so about a third. 22.9% is direct (Income Tax) and 10.7% indirect (VAT). Poorest fifth is 36.7% and richest 36.2%, which surprised me. The worked example for someone on £30,000 says they pay 16% on average (£4,879).
- How life changed: pay after inflation in 2025 was about the same as 2021 (£761 vs £767 a week). Housing: 65% of households in England own, 19% rent privately, average private rent £250 a week.
- I still don't really get what the Budget is. Nothing told me.
