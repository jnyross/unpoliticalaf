# I1 notes (52, self-employed plumber, Kent, self-assessment, distrusts statistics)

## What I found out, in my words

- Where the money goes: biggest chunk is pensions, benefits and social care, £33 of every £100 spent on services, then health at £21. Total £1,360bn in 2025-26. Fair enough, found it in seconds on the front page and again on the Spending page.
- Tax on a household: 33.6% of gross household income goes in tax on average (2023/24), 22.9% direct and 10.7% indirect like VAT. It says straight out this is an average across everyone, not my tax rate. Poorest fifth 36.7%, richest fifth 36.2%, which surprised me.
- Pay since I was young: pay after inflation in April 2025 (£767 a week) is about the same as April 2021 (£761). Housing: 65% of households in England own their home, 19% rent privately, up from 14% in 2008-09. The housing graph shows renting from the council fell and private renting rose.
- Tax history: taxes took 38.1% in 1977 on the old method and 33.6% now on the new one, but they say the two are not comparable. So I can't really say if it went up or down. Honest, but frustrating.
- I did not find anything about the self-employed. The only worked example is an employee on £30,000, which is not me (no Class 2/4 NI, no payments on account).

## What confused me, slowed me down or nearly misled me

- Front page, "Public money: four questions": the "Where does public money go" card says "General public services £13, of which £11 is interest", then a line about the OBR and IFS quoting a lower figure that subtracts interest received. I could not follow this and it made me suspicious.
- Front page, same card: £1,360bn total but "£1,228bn split by area; the rest is accounting adjustments". I do not know what "accounting adjustments" are, and it sounds like money being hidden.
- Front page and Taxes card: "Income Tax, National Insurance and VAT raised 65%" and "Income Tax 30%" next to "33.6% of household income" and "top 1% owed 27.2%". Lots of percentages of different things next to each other; easy to mix up. The page itself has a section "Three numbers that are easy to mix up", which tells me it is a real risk.
- Front page, "Who pays?" card: the headline 27.2% is the top 1%'s share of Income Tax, which is not what I asked, and it's from 2023 to 2024 while the others are 2025-26. Mixed years.
- Tax and income page, top: 33.6% is a big headline number but is "not anyone's tax rate", so it does not answer "how much of MY income goes on tax". I had to read on to the worked example.
- Tax and income page: the worked example is an employee on £30,000 with tax year 2026 to 2027. It is labelled "made-up", which I liked, but it does not cover self-employed people, so I could not use it.
- Tax and income page, worked example: "8% of weekly pay between £242 and £967" for NI is jargon-heavy and I did not follow how it came to £1,393.
- Tax and income page, "Why the same tax can look bigger or smaller": the cash-and-prices line "pay up 50%, prices up 40%, buys about 7% more (1.50 ÷ 1.40 ≈ 1.07)... Made-up numbers" is dropped in the middle of a paragraph about indirect tax; it looked like it belonged to something else and the maths in brackets put me off.
- Tax and income page, history section: two different methods (old to 2017/18, new from 2018/19) that "are not joined" so the 1977 vs now comparison I wanted cannot be made. The sentence under the chart title ("fell in 1978, rose until 1983, then fell until 2017/18; on the current method it fell until 2020/21, rose in 2021/22, then fell...") is very long and hard to follow on a phone.
- Tax and income page, chart: the 40 / 20 / 0 scale and the "Method changed" and "Covid" markers are small and hard to read on a phone; I could not see the break clearly.
- Pay after inflation page: the headline £761 to £767 uses "April 2025 prices" and "middle full-time employee, before tax". As a self-employed man I do not have a weekly wage, and "full-time employee" excludes people like me. Not said clearly.
- Pay after inflation page: the earlier stretches (£735 to £743, £755 to £764 and so on) are separate periods that cannot be joined, so I could not tell what happened since I was young (the 1990s), only since 2002.
- Pay after inflation page, chart: the vertical scale starts at 600, not zero (they say so in small print), so the lines look more dramatic than they are. Also lines are broken in four places with labels like "Jobs recoded" that mean nothing to me.
- Pay after inflation page: the chart text mentions "CPIH, the official consumer prices index that includes housing costs" in small grey print; I do not know what CPIH is, and I distrust official price indices anyway.
- Pay after inflation page: the page I landed on opened part-way down ("#key-answer") with the navigation menu cut off at the top, so I did not see the title bar for a moment and was not sure which page I was on.
- Pay after inflation page: the first screen is mostly a headline with the page's own navigation bar half off the screen (only "Debt, Year by year, Glossary, Sources" visible with no logo), which looked odd.
- Housing page: the four small charts have the same grey lines behind each, with years "1981 / 2024" squashed; dotted sections and a dashed gap around 2007 are not explained until you read the small print ("Labour Force Survey" vs "English Housing Survey").
- Housing page: England only, while the headline pages say UK. I live in Kent, so fine, but it was not obvious at first glance.
- Housing page: the figures (£250 rent a week, £242 mortgage) are "cash, at the time" and not adjusted, so no like-for-like comparison with 1981 can be made.
- Every page: long strings of dotted-underline words (GDP, public sector, indirect taxes, basic rate) look like links but are glossary pop-ups, and I was never sure whether tapping them would take me away from the page.
- Every page: repeated "More on ..." and "What the chart shows, in words" collapsed boxes hid the sources, so I could not check where the numbers come from, which matters to me as I distrust statistics.
- Front page: tiny "Quick answers" buttons (Women's employment, Pay after inflation, Class sizes, Holidays, Renting) have no button for tax, and the cards under "four questions" are the way to the tax answer; I nearly missed that the tax answer was on a separate page called "Tax and income" because the top menu says "Taxes" and "Who pays" too.
- Top menu: "Taxes", "Who pays" and "Tax and income" sound like the same thing, and I wasn't sure which one answered "how much of my income goes on tax".
- Menu label: "Year by year" does not tell me it is the life-changes section (pay, housing, holidays).
