## What I found out

1. **Where most public money goes.** The home page says the public sector spent £1,360bn in 2025-26. Of every £100 spent on services, £33 goes on social protection (pensions, benefits, social care), £21 on health, £13 on "general public services", which includes debt interest. So pensions and benefits are the biggest chunk, then the NHS.
2. **How much of household income goes on tax.** About a third: 33.6% of gross household income in 2023/24, on average. 22.9% is direct tax (like Income Tax) and 10.7% is indirect (like VAT). The poorest fifth (36.7%) and richest fifth (36.2%) pay about the same share, but in different taxes. I didn't get as far as what I'd pay on my own pay; there's a link to a £30,000 example I didn't open.
3. **How work has changed since I was young.** 75.3% of people aged 16-64 were in work in April-June 2025. The chart goes back to 1971 and the lowest point was 65.6% in 1983. The gap between men's and women's employment shrank from 39 points to 6. Manufacturing went from 24.9% of jobs in 1978 to 6.7% in 2026. I also looked at housing: 65% of households in England owned their home in 2024-25, and 19% rent privately (14% in 2008-09). The average private rent is £250 a week, against £242 for a mortgage. I couldn't tell what that means for someone my age.
