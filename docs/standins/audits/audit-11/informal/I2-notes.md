# I2 notes (23, Bristol grad, first job, no interest in politics; phone, 14 of 40 actions used)

## What I found out, in my words

1. Where most public money goes: pensions, benefits and social care. The home page says £33 of every £100 goes on that, then health (£21), then "general public services" (£13). Total is £1,360bn. Easy to find.
2. How much of household income goes on tax: 33.6% on average in 2023/24 (22.9% direct like Income Tax, 10.7% indirect like VAT). Poorest fifth 36.7%, richest fifth 36.2%, which surprised me. It says it is an average, not my own rate, and links to a made-up £30,000 example, which I did not open.
3. How life has changed: I tried holidays and housing. Holidays abroad were about 41m a year in 2008, about 58m in 2019, crashed in 2021, and were back to 58.4m (86 per 100 people) in 2025. Housing: 14% rented privately in 2008-09, 19% in 2024-25; owning with a mortgage fell. I could not tell what it was like when I was a kid (the charts start in 2008), so I only half answered "since I was young".

## Things that confused me, slowed me down or nearly misled me

- Home page: "£33 of every £100 spent on services (£1,228bn; the rest is accounting adjustments)" made me stop; I did not know what "accounting adjustments" are or why £1,228bn is not £1,360bn.
- Home page: the £1,360bn is called spent, then the next card says £1,105bn raised, and borrowing "£134bn"; the numbers do not obviously add up and I did not know if that gap matters.
- Home page: "General public services (incl. debt interest)" is vague jargon; I did not know what it means.
- Home page: the top 1% card shows 27.2% in big type, which reads like a headline about the rich but is only Income Tax, not all tax.
- Home page: the "Who pays" card and the "household tax" card sound alike; I was unsure which one answers "how much do I pay".
- household-tax.html: 33.6% is an average for everyone, so I could not tell whether it applies to someone on my pay; the pointer to the £30,000 example is easy to miss in a grey-text paragraph.
- household-tax.html: "gross household income", "private households" and "fifth of people" are dotted-underline jargon words that look like links; I was not sure whether tapping them leaves the page.
- household-tax.html: "On this page: 4 sections" is a collapsed box that I almost skipped, and it holds the only table of contents.
- life/index.html: the page promises "every year's figures" but I had to pick one of five topic buttons; the year dropdown with 55 choices (1971 to 2025) is confusing as it has no obvious purpose next to the topics.
- life/index.html#holidays: the small sparkline on the overview had a gap and dashed lines with no labels on the phone, and I could not read what it meant without the small print below.
- life/index.html#holidays: the heading link says "Holidays in every year since 1980" but the chart on the holidays page starts in 2008, which nearly misled me into expecting 1980 data.
- life/holidays.html: the intro box is a long paragraph of figures (2019, 2020, 2021, Spain, France, Italy), too much to skim; the answer to "has it changed" is further down in the chart title.
- life/holidays.html: the chart label "New 58.4 million" overlaps the line and is cut off at the right edge on my phone screen.
- life/holidays.html: the notes about "line breaks at 2024 (new method)" and "2025 (fully new method)" mean I cannot trust comparing recent years with older ones, and it is not clear which numbers I can compare.
- life/holidays.html: "per 100 residents" being "an average, not a typical experience" was a useful warning but confusing wording.
- life/housing.html: the four small charts in a grid are hard to read on a phone (tiny labels, grey lines behind), and the 2008 start means nothing about my childhood.
- life/housing.html: the headline says "England" households while the rest of the site says UK, which I only noticed because it says it in the first box.
- All pages: the main menu has seven items in two rows; I was not sure which of "Spending", "Taxes", "Who pays" and "Tax and income" was the one for my question.
- **blocking** (partly): I could not find any figure from when I was young (about 2000) for holidays or housing in the first screens I looked at; the charts I saw start at 2008, so "how has it changed since I was young" was only partly answerable from what I saw (I did not try the year picker or Compare years).
