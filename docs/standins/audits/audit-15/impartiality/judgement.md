# Impartiality judgement: audit 15

Judge: neutral stand-in. I did not build the site. Inputs: `critic-left.md` (9 points), `critic-right.md` (10 points), `docs/standins/impartiality-rule.md`. I checked every point against the built pages in `site/` (version 4cb1bcc).

Overall: the critics often attack the same choice from opposite sides: the "68 of the 78 years" chart title, the per-household debt line, the "little change from 2010-11 to 2019-20" summary, the 2021 pay base and the forecast receipts share. In each of these cases the page follows a mechanical, published rule, and I reject both sides. No point shows a breach that would give a newcomer a picture of the facts that the official evidence does not support. Four points show real but small imbalances.

## Breaches found by the judge that the critics missed

I checked start years, chart scales and group order across the main pages (`index`, `spending`, `spending-areas`, `spending-history`, `taxes`, `tax-history`, `who-pays`, `who-pays-age`, `who-pays-households`, `household-tax`, `borrowing`, `international`, and `life/` index, work, earnings, housing, schools, compare 2011-2019):

- **Start years.** Every long comparison starts at the first year of its series: OBR databank 1948-49 and the deflator at 1955-56, PESA 2003-04, OECD tax 1965 and spending 2007, IMF debt 1990, net interest 2007, LFS 1971, EHS 2008-09, ASHE segments. The spending-by-area window is the fixed ten years. No change is worked out across a marked break. No breach.
- **Scales.** Every horizontal bar list I checked is proportional from zero. Every line chart whose axis starts above zero says so in its caption ("The scale starts at 35/600/60/15/20…, not at zero"). The small-multiple panels state "All panels use the same scale". No breach.
- **Group order.** Income fifths, age bands, household types, nations and English regions keep official order. One-year category charts are ordered largest first. Country charts are rankings and say so. No breach.
- **New finding (minor).** In `international.html`, section "The UK beside the other G7 countries", the visible lead sentence and bar chart show only four of the seven G7 countries (France, the UK, the United States and Japan), and nothing says how they were chosen. The UK is shown above two of the three others. Italy and Germany, both higher spenders, and Canada, a lower one, appear only inside the collapsed "More" panel. There the UK ranks 4th of 7, which is the middle. The visible selection slightly overstates how high UK spending is among the G7. Fix: show all seven, as the collapsed panel already does, or state the selection rule.
