# I1 notes (P3, self-employed plumber, Kent) - 12 actions used

## What I found out (in my words)
- Where the money goes: about £1,360bn in 2025-26. Biggest bit is pensions, benefits and social care, £33 of every £100. Health £21, general public services £13 (most of that debt interest).
- Tax share of household income: 33.6% on average in 2023/24. Poorest fifth 36.7%, richest fifth 36.2%, so it is about the same all the way up. Not my tax rate, they say.
- How life changed (housing): 65% of English households own their home (2024-25). Private renting is 19%, up from 14% in 2008-09. Pay after inflation in April 2025 was about the same as April 2021.

## Confusions
- Home page, Spending card: "OBR and IFS often quote a lower figure that subtracts interest received" - jargon, and I do not know who they are, so I did not trust the total.
- Home page, Spending card: £1,360bn versus "HM Treasury splits £1,228bn by area" - two totals, I could not tell which is the real one.
- Home page, Spending card: "£33 of every £100 ... £21 ... £13" adds to far less than £100 and the other lines are hidden, so it looked incomplete.
- Home page, Tax card: 33.6% average, yet "not anyone's tax rate", which made the headline number feel useless to me as a self-assessed tradesman.
- Home page, Tax card: lowest fifth pays more (36.7%) than the highest (36.2%) - surprising, mostly VAT, explained only further down; nearly misled me into thinking the rich pay less.
- Home page, Earnings card: "pay went from £610 to £767" in cash then "£761 to £767" after inflation in the same card - two pairs of numbers, easy to mix up.
- Home page, Housing card: 65% owned but no earlier figure on the card to compare, so "how has it changed" needed another tap.
- Home page, Holidays card: headline is 58.4 million visits but the chart is per 100 residents and stops in 2023, so the chart does not match the number.
- Home page: the page is very long and the four question cards plus a second list of cards look similar; I scrolled past the "how life changed" cards without knowing they were a separate section.
- Housing page: chart shows "Own outright" 35.5% etc. with dotted early years and a break line at 2007, unexplained until the small print; hard to read on a phone.
- Housing page: headline box compares 2024-25 only to 2008-09, while the chart below starts at 1981 - not "since I was young".
