# I2 notes (23, Bristol, first job, phone)

## What I found out
- Where the money goes: the UK public sector spent £1,360bn in 2025-26. The biggest chunk is "social protection" (pensions, benefits, social care): about £33 of every £100. Health is £21. Debt interest is £11 (it sits inside "general public services", £13). Found on the home page and the Spending page.
- Tax on my income: on average 33.6% of household income goes on tax (2023/24), roughly a third. It is split into 22.9% direct (like Income Tax) and 10.7% indirect (like VAT). The page says it is an average, not my own rate. Surprise: the poorest fifth pay 36.7%, more than the middle (30.2%), mostly VAT. Found on the home page card and the Tax and income page.
- How life has changed (housing, as I rent): in 2024-25 19% of English households rented privately, up from 14% in 2008-09. Average private rent is £250 a week. Back in 1981, 57% owned and 11% rented privately. Owning with a mortgage fell. After inflation, rents dipped, then rose, so not a simple "rents exploded" story. Found on Housing page.
