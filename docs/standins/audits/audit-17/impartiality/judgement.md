## Breaches the critics missed

Checked across the main pages: the start years of every comparison, the chart scales (every bar starts at zero, and each line chart that does not start at zero says so: spending share of GDP from 35, productivity from 90, home and life small charts), and the order of groups (fifths, age bands and nations are in official order; one-year category charts are largest first).

1. **defence.html: start year 2010-11. Material.** The comparison paragraph reads: "Defence spending was £65.4bn in 2025-26, up from £39.3bn in 2010-11 … Its share of GDP was lower in 2025-26 than in 2010-11: 2.1% against 2.4%." The series starts in 2003-04, and the rule's window for spending by area is ten years (2015-16, which spending.html uses). 2010-11 fits neither rule. It is also the high point of the share series. On the rule's ten-year window, the share of GDP was *higher* (1.9% to 2.1%) and real spending rose 29%, the opposite of the page's "lower". A newcomer would take away the wrong direction of change.
2. **welfare.html: comparison across its own declared break. Minor.** Every chart breaks at 2020-21 ("values either side are not compared"), yet the lead sets 1955-56 against 2021-22, and pensioners' share is given as "54% in 2021-22, against 59% in 1978-79". household-tax.html shows the right way to handle this. The comparison runs in no particular political direction.
3. **Sourcing contrary to the published principle. Minor.** methods.html says "Official sources only", and every page footer says "built only from official UK statistics". economy-options.html ranks Resolution Foundation, CBI (a draft) and ENA/BEAMA figures. Each figure is labelled on the page, so this is not material. It overlaps with R5.
