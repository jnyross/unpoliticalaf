## What I found out, in my own words

**Where most of the money goes.** The government spent £1,360bn in 2025-26. Out of every £100 spent on services, £33 goes on pensions, benefits and social care, and £21 on health. Then £13 on "general public services", and £11 of that is interest on debt. So the biggest slice is pensions and benefits, health second.

**How much of a household's income goes on tax.** About a third: 33.6% of gross household income in 2023/24, on average. 22.9% is direct tax like Income Tax and 10.7% is indirect, like VAT. It says that is an average and not anyone's own rate. The poorest fifth paid 36.7% and the richest fifth 36.2%, which surprised me, though it says most of the poorest group's tax is VAT.

**How housing has changed since I was young.** In 2024-25, 65% of households in England owned their home and 19% rented privately. Back in 1981 owning outright was about 25% and now it is about 35%. Renting from the council fell and private renting went up, from about 10% to nearly 19%. Average private rent is £250 a week. The site says nothing about why it changed. I note it is England only, not Scotland.

**Pay and work (looked at in passing).** After inflation, pay in 2025 was about the same as in 2021. The share of people working was 72.0% in 1971 and 75.3% in 2025.
