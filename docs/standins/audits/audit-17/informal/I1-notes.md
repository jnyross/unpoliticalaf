# I1 notes (plumber, 52, Kent, self-assessment) - 24 actions used

## What I found out, in my words

**Where most public money goes.** Pensions, benefits and social care, the biggest slice. About £33 out of every £100 spent on services. Health is next at £21. The total is £1,360bn for 2025-26. (Spending page and front page agree.)

**How much of a household's income goes on tax.** 33.6% of gross household income on average, 2023/24. Of that, 22.9% is direct tax like Income Tax and 10.7% is indirect like VAT. It says straight out this is an average across everyone, not anyone's own rate. The poorest fifth pay 36.7% and the richest fifth 36.2%, so it's much the same up and down the scale, which surprised me. Whether that is my rate, I can't tell.

**How pay has changed.** Middle full-time worker: £761 a week in April 2021, £767 in April 2025, in today's prices. So after prices, roughly flat (+0.8%). Housing: 65% of households in England own their home, 19% rent privately; average private rent £250 a week, mortgage £242. In 2008-09 14% rented privately. I never found a number for when I was young.
