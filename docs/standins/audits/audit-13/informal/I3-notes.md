# I3 notes: 52, self-employed plumber, Kent, distrusts statistics

14 of 40 actions used, on a phone.

## What I found out, in my words

1. Where most of the money goes. Home page, first card. The government spent £1,360bn in 2025-26. Out of every £100 on services, £33 goes on pensions and benefits, £21 on health, and £13 on "general public services", which is mostly interest on the debt. So pensions and benefits first, then the NHS. Fair enough, though I would want to see who counted it.
2. How much of a household's income goes on tax. Home page, 4th card. 33.6% of gross household income on average in 2023/24. The poorest fifth pay 36.7% and the richest fifth 36.2%, so it is roughly a third for everyone, and the poorest mostly pay it through VAT and the like. It says outright that this is an average and "not anyone's tax rate", which I liked. I did not find what a self-employed person like me pays.
3. How life has changed. Year by year page, comparing 1990 with 2025.
   - Work: 72.8% of people aged 16 to 64 in work in 1990, 75.3% now.
   - Pay after inflation: it would not compare 1990 with 2025 because the figures only start in 2002. It offered 2011 against 2020 (+1.0%) instead. On the home page, pay was about the same in 2021 and 2025 (£761 against £767 a week).
   - Housing: it would not compare 1990 with 2025 either. 65% of households in England owned their home in 2024-25.
   - So I did not get a straight "then versus now" for pay or housing since I was young. I got work and a "can't say".

- Home page, the same card: the bars show only three areas, and I could not see where defence, schools and police were without tapping through.
- Home page, spending card: "General public services (incl. debt interest)" looks like a vague catch-all, and the £11 of the £13 being interest was easy to miss.
- Home page, household tax card: the lowest fifth pay 36.7% and the highest 36.2%, which looks wrong at first glance and I distrusted it. The explanation about indirect tax is cut off further down the card.
- Home page, household tax card: the year is 2023/24 while the spending is 2025-26, so the years do not match up, and I had to read carefully to notice.

- Home page, Year by year intro: "Pick any year from 1971 to 2025... Work, earnings, housing... from 2008, holidays from 2009" is a dense paragraph, and it tells me the figures I want do not go back far.
- Home page, small charts: "the small charts do not start at zero" and the dashed lines and gaps need explaining before they mean anything, so I would not trust the squiggles.
