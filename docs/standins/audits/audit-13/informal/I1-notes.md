# I1 notes (nurse, Cardiff, phone, 13 actions used of 40)

## What I found out, in my words
- Where the money goes: the biggest chunk is pensions, benefits and social care, about £33 of every £100 spent on services. Health is £21 (that is the NHS, my bit). Then "general public services" £13, which is mostly interest on debt. Total was £1,360bn in 2025-26.
- Tax: on the front page, 33.6% of household income goes on tax on average, 2023/24. It says that is an average, not my rate. Lowest and highest fifths are about 36-37%, the middle about 30%. I did not find what it would mean for a nurse's pay packet.
- Housing since I was young: 65% of households in England owned their home in 2024-25. Owning outright has gone up since 1981, owning with a mortgage has gone down, private renting has roughly doubled (about 9% to 19%). Private rent about £250 a week.

## Confusing, slowing, or nearly misleading
- Home page: "£1,228bn of this by area... the rest is accounting entries it does not split" and "net figure the OBR and IFS quote" is jargon I skimmed past; the key £33 comes only after a dense paragraph.
- Home page, "General public services (£13) includes debt interest (£11 of the £13)" sits in a list of "services", which nearly misled me into thinking £13 was like a department.
- Home page, tax card: 33.6% sits next to the "lowest fifth 36.7%" which is higher than the middle, so the bars looked wrong until I read the explanation lower down (indirect tax like VAT).
- Home page, tax card: it is all households' gross income, so it did not answer "how much tax do I pay"; I would have liked a pay example. Not found, but I got the 33.6% answer, so not blocking.
- Home page, everyday-life section: the intro paragraph is long and lists start years (1971, 1981, 1980, 2002, 2006) that I could not use.
- Home page, small charts: "do not start at zero", "dashed line means definition changed", and dotted underlined words (GDP, G7, borrowing) look like links but are glossary terms, which slowed me.
- Home page, Work card: 75.1% in work with a flat-looking line; I could not tell whether that is good or bad or what changed since I was young.
- Home page, Earnings card: "£761 to £767 ... about the same" but also "in cash £610 to £767" gave me two stories; the "last changed how it codes jobs" note was confusing.
- Home page, Holidays card: the headline is 58.4 million visits in 2025 but the chart ends at 2023 and is per 100 residents, so number and chart do not match.
- Housing page, top box: "social rent £129 and average mortgage payment £242 (cash, at the time)" mixes cash and later after-inflation figures without telling me which to trust.
- Housing page, ownership chart: four small panels with tiny axis labels, a gap before 2008 and a vertical dashed line; I had to read the caption to learn the survey changed, so I could not compare either side of 2008.
- Housing page: "Compare two years of housing" link and chart buttons (Work, Earnings, Housing...) appear after much scrolling, so I did not know how to compare my childhood year with now until near the end.
- Housing page: "More on ... table, limits and sources" and "What the chart shows, in words" boxes are collapsed many times, so lots of vertical clutter on a phone.
- Housing page, costs chart: the "Private rent" and "Mortgage payment" labels overlap on the lines and are hard to read.
- Housing page: "Change is not cause" box was useful but I did not understand why it was there until the end.
