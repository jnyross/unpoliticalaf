Where the UK's public money goes

In 2025-26 the UK public sector spent £1,360bn. This page shows what it went on, and how that has changed. It uses the government's own figures.

The figures are from HM Treasury, Public Expenditure Statistical Analyses 2026, out on 2026-07-16. PESA treats 2025-26 as outturn; it can be revised. Tap a number, or use Tab then Enter, to see its source. "bn" means billion.

Quick answers

What does the government spend the most on?

Pensions, benefits and social care: £407bn in 2025-26. See the split.

Out of every £100 spent on services, how much goes on health?

£21. See every area.

How much goes on interest on government debt?

£130bn in 2025-26 (PESA outturn, subject to revision), of which £96.9bn is interest on loans to the government. See what is in it.

Has health spending gone up in the last ten years, once rising prices are taken into account?

Yes. It was £192bn in 2015-16 (in 2025-26 prices) and £258bn in 2025-26: 34% more. See the chart.

Is total spending a bigger share of the economy than ten years ago?

Yes. 44.3% of GDP in 2025-26, compared with 40.8% in 2015-16. See every ten years since 1945.

How much goes on pensions, compared with defence?

Pensions paid in old age: £155bn. Defence: £65.4bn. See the selected items.

Which numbers are forecasts?

The Treasury totals after 2025-26 are forecasts. They have a dashed line and the word "Forecast". Earlier Treasury years are outturn and can be revised. The OBR series uses its own vintage; see the source notes.

Where can I check a number myself?

Tap or point at any number. You will see the table and cell it comes from. How this site works links to each file we use.

Interest on government debt

The Treasury's line for debt interest in 2025-26 is £130bn. That is £11 of every £100. Not all of it is interest on loans. Here is what it is made of:

  • Central government debt interest: £96.9bn
  • Local government debt interest: £1.1bn
  • Public corporation debt interest: £0.5bn
  • Bank of England: £12.3bn
  • Public sector pensions: £19.4bn

The Bank of England item covers its bond-buying scheme (quantitative easing) and a related lending scheme. Source: HM Treasury, Public Expenditure Statistical Analyses 2026, Table 5.2, notes 2 and 3.

Every ten years since 1945

These charts step back ten years at a time from 2025-26, to 1945-46, the first year after the Second World War. They always show each amount as a share of GDP. That is the fairest way to compare years so far apart.

Spending and income

In 1945-46, spending was 55.1% of GDP. In 2025-26 it was 44.5%. When spending is more than income, the government borrows the gap.
