Taxes and other receipts

In 2025-26, the UK public sector received £1,231.3bn in taxes and other receipts. This is the latest outturn in these tables. “bn” means billion. The financial year runs from April to March. Cash totals are rounded to one decimal place.

Not all receipts are tax. Taxes on the source's National Accounts basis were £1,104.7bn. Other receipts were £126.6bn.

On the same OBR basis, spending was £1,365.6bn, borrowing was £134.3bn, and debt at end March was £2,916.8bn.

Source: Office for Budget Responsibility, Public finances databank, October 2026. No next release date is announced in the retained evidence.

Outturn can be revised. The outturn figures are consistent with the public finances bulletin released on 22 September 2026.

That bulletin date is not the later forecast vintage. Forecast years are shown separately below.

What are the main taxes?

Our summary of official guidance

Income Tax

Income Tax applies to taxable income. Not all income is taxable. Income Tax guidance: Overview

National Insurance

National Insurance has its own rules for work and self-employment. Contributions help you qualify for certain benefits and the State Pension. They are not a personal savings pot. National Insurance guidance: Overview and Who pays

VAT

VAT is a tax in many purchase prices, not all. Goods and services can have different rates, be zero-rated or be exempt. VAT guidance: Overview; What VAT is charged on; How much VAT you must charge

Corporation Tax

Corporation Tax applies to taxable company or association profits. Corporation Tax guidance: Overview

This site’s Corporation Tax receipts group also includes related charges. See the OBR group scope and source note. OBR Receipts (£bn): U4 and note C42

The Personal Allowance applies to Income Tax, not every tax. You can owe no Income Tax and still pay VAT through purchases. Personal Allowance: allowances and reliefs

National Insurance has its own rules and payment periods. Being below the annual Income Tax allowance does not by itself mean you owe no National Insurance. National Insurance: Who pays

Where you live can change the rules

Scotland sets Income Tax rates and bands on non-savings, non-dividend income of Scottish taxpayers. Those powers exclude the Personal Allowance; HMRC collects the tax. Scottish Government: Income Tax powers

Wales also has devolved Income Tax rates. Where you live matters. Savings and dividends follow UK rules, outside these devolved rate powers. Welsh rates: Who pays

These are not personal tax calculations or a guide to every devolved tax.

Our explanation

Income tax
PAYE, self-assessed and other income tax are added with their source signs. Other income tax can be negative.
National Insurance contributions
National Insurance is shown as its own source series.
VAT including refunds (net VAT plus refunds)
This gross grouping is a source-based presentation, not household VAT burden. The notes do not explicitly establish how refunds are classified within National Accounts taxes. The net VAT and refund rows are separate below.
Corporation tax and related charges
Adds the source's onshore and offshore corporation tax rows. The onshore label includes Bank Surcharge and the Electricity Generator Levy (EGL). OBR note 3 also includes residential property developers tax and Pillar 2 taxes. Petroleum revenue tax, energy profits levy and bank levy stay in other taxes.
Other National Accounts taxes
A calculated remainder after the named groups. It draws partly on a mixed source line that is not itemised. Arithmetic closure does not prove the classification of each tax or refund.
Receipts outside National Accounts taxes
A calculated difference: all receipts minus National Accounts taxes. It is not the mixed source row relabelled as non-tax. Do not add the source's interest, dividend or operating surplus rows again.

How receipts, spending and borrowing fit

Using matching OBR Aggregates (£bn) rows: spending £1,365.6bn minus receipts £1,231.3bn equals £134.3bn. The source's borrowing row is £134.3bn. Borrowing is not tax or receipts. It is the annual gap on this accrual basis. Negative borrowing means receipts were greater than spending.

The spending page uses PESA. That is a different vintage and presentation. Its spending total is not the OBR total used in this balance. Do not subtract receipts here from PESA spending.

Debt is a stock at end March. Borrowing is a flow over a year. Changes in debt need not equal borrowing. Published debt is £2,916.8bn, or 93.3% of GDP. Debt uses March-centred GDP, not annual GDP. We retain the published share; we do not replace it with a ratio worked out from the stock.

Does this show who pays?

This page does not show who pays. These tables show receipts by tax type. They do not show how payments are split between people, or who ultimately bears the cost. They give no percentages for who bears that cost.

Who pays tax? shows separate Income Tax and household views. Receipts and household tax costs are different measures.

OBR note (original wording): Glossary!C5
Revenue relating to activities in the current year, comprising mainly direct and indirect taxes, but also including social security contributions, interest, dividends, capital taxes and profits from trading activities.
OBR note (original wording): Glossary!C11
The difference between total public sector receipts and expenditure on an accrued basis each year. As the widest measure of borrowing it is a key indicator of the fiscal position. PSNB is the headline measure of 'the deficit'.
OBR note (original wording): Glossary!C14
A stock measure of the public sector's net liability position i.e. its liabilities minus its liquid assets. It is broadly the stock equivalent of public sector net borrowing, but measured on a cash rather than an accrued basis. It is also the fiscal measure used for the Government's supplementary fiscal target. PSND is the key measure of the country's overall debt.
OBR note (original wording): Glossary!C15
A measure of the amount of cash which is needed to make up the difference betweeen spending and revenues. This is different from net borrowing as it also includes financial transactions, which will affect the cash position but are not included in net borrowing. Public sector net debt is driven by changes in the public sector net cash requirement.
