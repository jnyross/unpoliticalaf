FUNDING A FAIR DEAL
The Liberal Democrat manifesto is a bold, ambitious and fully-costed plan to
give the British people the fair deal they deserve. And we will pay for it fairly,
too.


After years of Conservative chaos and neglect, there is no doubt that our
NHS, social care, schools and other public services desperately need more
investment. GPs are overstretched, hospitals are overcrowded and school
buildings are crumbling.


At the same time, families and pensioners are struggling in the face of a
cost-of-living crisis. The Conservatives have already put up their taxes by
freezing income tax thresholds. It would be wrong to ask them to pay more
to clear up the Conservatives’ mess.


That’s why we will fund our fair deal without raising income tax, national
insurance contributions or VAT. Instead, we will focus on tax changes that
make the system fairer – such as reversing the Conservatives’ tax cuts for
the big banks and imposing a proper windfall tax on the super-proﬁts of oil
and gas companies.


Responsible public ﬁnances are essential for the stability, certainty and
conﬁdence that drive economic growth, and for getting mortgage rates
under control. The Conservative Government has shown all too clearly the
damage and pain caused by ﬁscal irresponsibility. Under our plans, we will
get the national debt falling as a share of the economy and ensure that day-
to-day spending does not exceed the amount raised in taxes.

1. End the crisis in the NHS and social care: 8,000 more GPs; invest in
   cancer research and treatment; guarantee access to an NHS dentist for
   everyone needing urgent and emergency care; invest in mental health
   services, including talking therapies and walk-in hubs for young people;
   make chronic mental health prescriptions free; increase the public
   health grant; supervised toothbrushing for children; scrap VAT on
   children’s toothbrushes and toothpaste; increase the number of staﬀed
   hospital beds; introduce free personal care; introduce a higher Carer’s
   Minimum Wage.
2. Support unpaid carers: Boost Carer’s Allowance by £20 per week, raise
   the amount carers can earn and introduce an earnings taper to end the
   unfair cliﬀ edge.
3. Invest in early years and childcare: Give disadvantaged three- and
   four-year-olds an extra ﬁve free hours a week; increase the Early Years
   Pupil Premium; increase Statutory Maternity Pay, Shared Parental Pay
   and paternity pay; introduce a use-it-or-lose-it month of leave for fathers
   and partners; pay an enhanced rate of Child Beneﬁt for one-year-olds;
   introduce paid neonatal care leave.
4. Invest in schools: Put a dedicated mental health professional in every
   school; extend free school meals to all children in poverty; introduce a
   Tutoring Guarantee; fund teacher training properly; introduce a Young
   People’s Premium; extend Pupil Premium Plus funding to children in
   kinship care; reduce the amount schools pay towards EHCPs; expand
   provision of extracurricular activities.
5. Invest in higher education and lifelong learning: Reinstate
   maintenance grants; create Lifelong Skills Grants.
6. Tackle child poverty and make the welfare system fairer: Scrap the
   two-child limit, the beneﬁt cap and the bedroom tax; reverse cuts to
   bereavement support; restore the full rate of Universal Credit for all
   parents regardless of age. Includes Housing Beneﬁt and Local Housing
   Allowance savings from the increase in social housing.
7. Support farmers, rural communities and the environment: Boost
   British farming; provide more funding for the Environment Agency and
   Natural England; expand Rural Fuel Duty Relief.

8. Cut the cost of transport: Reintroduce the plug-in car grant; cut VAT on
   public charging; extend half fares on buses, trams and trains; introduce
   a Young Person’s Buscard.
9. International, defence and borders funding: Reverse Conservative
   cuts to Army troop numbers; return international development
   spending to 0.7% of GNI.
10. Other: Support small employers with Statutory Sick Pay costs; establish
   creative enterprise zones; end period poverty; reform the Child
   Maintenance Service.
11. Additional funding for Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland: An
   additional £1,480m for Scotland, £760m for Wales and £650m for
   Northern Ireland would be available for them to spend on their
   priorities.
12. Cut the asylum backlog and allow asylum seekers to work after
   three months: Extra tax and national insurance revenue, savings in
   asylum support and accommodation and healthcare spending.
13. Crack down on Conservative waste by halving consultancy
   spending: Halve the amount the government spends on external
   consultants.

14. Reverse Conservative cuts to bank taxes: Reverse Conservative tax
   cuts for the big banks, restoring Bank Surcharge and Bank Levy
   revenues to 2016 levels in real terms.
15. Fairly reform Capital Gains Tax: Close loopholes exploited by the
   super-wealthy by adjusting the rates and basing them solely on capital
   gains while increasing the tax-free allowance from £3,000 to £5,000, on
   top of a new tax-free allowance for inﬂation, and introducing a relief for
   small businesses.
16. A proper windfall tax on oil & gas super-proﬁts: Scrap the
   ‘investment allowance’ loophole, increase the headline rate and extend it
   to proﬁts since October 2021 when Liberal Democrats ﬁrst called for its
   introduction.
17. Raise the Digital Services Tax on tech giants: Increase the Digital
   Services Tax on social media ﬁrms and other tech giants from 2% to 6%.
18. Introduce a 4% tax on share buybacks: Introduce a 4% tax on the
   share buyback schemes of FTSE-100 listed companies, to incentivise
   productive investment, job creation and economic growth.
19. Sewage tax on water company proﬁts: Apply an additional 16% tax on
   water company proﬁts.
20. Tobacco company proﬁts levy: Set up a new levy on tobacco company
   proﬁt margins with an inbuilt anti-avoidance mechanism.
21. Super tax on private jet ﬂights: Introduce a new tax on each private jet
   ﬂight and remove the VAT exemption for private ﬂights.
22. Fairly reform aviation taxes: Reform the taxation of international
   ﬂights to focus on those who ﬂy the most, while reducing costs for
   ordinary households who take one or two international return ﬂights
   per year.
23. Tackle tax avoidance and evasion: Narrow the £36 billion annual tax
   gap by investing an extra £1 billion a year in HMRC to improve customer
   support and boost compliance and anti-avoidance activities.

PUBLIC INVESTMENT
We will invest an additional £19.7 billion a year on average over the next
Parliament in public assets, to boost growth and improve our public
services.


This includes:
   £1.1 billion of investment in hospitals and other NHS infrastructure and
   equipment.
   £1.9 billion to build new schools and clearing the backlog of repairs, £0.9
   billion of which will be funded by redirecting capital funding for
   unnecessary new free schools.
   £8.4 billion to tackle climate change and protect the natural
   environment, including:
        • An emergency Home Energy Upgrade programme.
        • Investment in renewable power, grid infrastructure and energy
            storage.
        • Protecting habitats and wildlife, restoring peatlands and planting
            60 million trees a year.
   £1.2 billion of investment in better transport, including:
        • Rolling out more electric vehicle charging points.
        • Creating new cycling and walking networks.
        • Signiﬁcantly extending the electriﬁcation of the rail network.
   £6.2 billion to support our target of building 150,000 social homes a year
   and ensure that gigabit broadband is available to every home and
   business.
   An additional £1.9 billion would be made available for Scotland, Wales
   and Northern Ireland to spend on their priorities: £1 billion for Scotland,
   £0.5 billion for Wales, and £0.4 billion for Northern Ireland.
